## The Art of a Girlfriend-Friendly Bikepacking Trip 

Bikepacking.com hosts no shortage of routes, but it lacks information on whether a route is what I call “girlfriend friendly”. Eryn, my lovely partner, is kind enough to join me on some easy bikepacking trips, as I can no longer bikepack since breaking my ankle. She's a new cyclist who has never mountain biked, and she rides a Trek Checkpoint with 40c tires and 0 suspension mods. We quickly learned on our first overnighter, a steep and rugged loop in Nederland, that a route for her would need to be specially curated.

So, I started this section of my blog to share route beta for the novice bikepacker. These routes are short, picturesque, and on easy terrain. 

For my first installment of a novice-friendly route, I planned a gravel and tame singletrack overnighter in Vedauwoo.

## The Route:
We parked the car at the dirt lot on the intersection of Vedauwoo Glen Road and FS 700K. I’m fairly certain that overnight parking is allowed here, but I was relieved to arrive at a car the next day without any tickets. 

From the intersection, the track follows a rough washboard up Vedauwoo Glen Road and onto FS 700D, to the Reynolds Hill TH.

![Rider posing and pointing at the Reynolds Hill Trailhead sign beside a loaded gravel bike](https://i.imgur.com/80a4bEL.jpeg)

The washboarding proved to be the worst part of this trip. I knew it would be rough from previous climbing trips out there, but it is another beast entirely on a bike. 

I fared okay - my Cutthroat has a drop-seat and suspension stem, but it was far from enjoyable. Eryn, on the other hand, had a pretty rough go on the washboard, especially towards the end of the day. If you’re going to do this overnighter, I cannot recommend suspension enough.

Thankfully, we only had a couple miles of this to start. Unfortunately, the route starts and ends here. Look forward to this tomorrow.

The Reynolds Hill TH transitions onto a singletrack path called “Cow's Trail”. Strava and local maps mark this as “blue” difficulty, but it proved to be beautiful and easy. We dismounted a couple of times to cross streams and move around obstacles, otherwise enjoying the long, flat dirt track.

The track spit us out at the Blair picnic site, where we followed Blair-Walls Road out to the Lincoln Monument.

The monument is a little absurd. I got the vibe that Lincoln was a slumbering automaton, caged in stone, and waiting to be awoken. He remained asleep for our passage, a lonely sentinel on the highest point on I-80. 

The visitor center is a lovely little break spot with bathrooms, water, and a warm place to snack. It’s also a George A. Wyman waypoint! I wonder if Eryn and I will find our way back here when we take on Portland to Portland.

![Cyclist in a bright green jacket giving a thumbs-up to the George A. Wyman waypoint sign on a brick wall](https://i.imgur.com/DNJhWzF.jpeg)

But with the sun soon setting, we jumped back on the road to backtrack to Headquarters Road. In my “theory” GPX file, I provide a green singletrack alternative to the washboarded gravel. With the sun setting and temperature dropping, we decided not to take on any unfamiliar terrain, but I suggest giving it a try to avoid the washboard.

We made camp shortly after turning onto Twin Mountain Road, at a beautiful hill overlooking the Voo, and the highway perfectly backlit on the horizon. There was even some leftover firewood for us to enjoy in the morning.

![The sunset colors the sky pink over a gravel trail](https://i.imgur.com/uMvNZsB.jpeg)
![Silhouette of a truck on the highway against an orange sunset sky](https://i.imgur.com/1ZaoTqc.jpeg)

I highly recommend camping along this road. It has established sites, some firewood if you’re lucky, and it sits just before a big decision point. The only downside is the lack of water - make sure you fill up at the Monument. 

The next day, Eryn and I enjoyed a lovely sunrise and warm fire. Shortly down the road, I asked Eryn if she was up for the +10 miles and 1,000' vert detour up to the Upper North Crow Reservoir, or if she wanted to return straight to the car. We decided on the latter and turned onto an old doubletrack road. Much like the Cow's Trail, this was the best riding of the trip: a Checkpoint-friendly dirt trail of autumn leaves, cool trees, and a funky little beaver pond.

![Cyclist riding a dirt trail past a pond and autumn trees under a wide, streaky blue sky](https://i.imgur.com/OwzyIev.jpeg)

Unfortunately, the car could not be close enough as we re-emerged onto Vedauwoo Glen and the heinous washboard. I think Eryn would’ve wanted more mileage if not for this washboard – it really just takes it out of you. I'll say it again: if you’re a novice bikepacker or riding with your partner, bring some sort of suspension.

## Overview:
Overall, this route proved to be the perfect length and difficulty for Eryn. It's an excellent gateway into longer trips, staying on gravel and tame singletracks. We only had to dismount a few times to avoid obstacles on the gravel bikes. 

I do acknowledge that this track could easily be completed in a single day, especially unweighted. We really only rode for one evening and one morning as it was. But it serves as an excellent beginner course with the option for more miles if you feel strong on day 2.

As mentioned, I’ve provided two GPX routes here. One, we actually rode, and the other is my theorized route. If you do ride the longer version, let me know how it was! I’d love to know how trustworthy my Google Earth and Strava heatmap sources are.
